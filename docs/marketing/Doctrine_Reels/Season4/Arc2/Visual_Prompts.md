Same concept. Rahul now reads the Bible for himself, speaks when asked, and
walks with one younger believer — Arjun. One real moment per reel, five
frames a few seconds apart, then the brand outro. Generate each frame with
the previous frame attached.

Every prompt here follows the direction guide: each frame names its camera,
set, blocking and light; each clip names its transition type, one camera move,
three action beats, what must not change, and an ambient-only audio bed. Read
the **Staging** note at the top of each reel before generating — it is the map
the prompts are written against.

**Adults only.** Flow rejects anyone under 18, so every person in every frame is an adult.

**Rules that override composition** (from the scripts' production notes): a
Bible on screen is always a printed Bible — the reading never happens on a
phone. No street, stage, crowd, microphone, leaflet or door-to-door scene. No
other faith's place of worship, symbol, text, festival or dress, ever. No
food of any kind in the law reel; no property, plot or money in the promise
reel; no stones or sling in the David reel. No count of anything. The app,
its discipler feature and its groups never appear. Never Jesus' face; where
he is in a painting he is a distant figure from behind.

Paintings: the empty tomb with no figure (2·1), the apostles praying (2·2), the women at the tomb (2·4), Paul before Agrippa (2·5). Vikram carries 2·1 and 2·3; the elder in 2·4 is Rahul's own uncle — warm, never an opponent.

Timing is unchanged: five clips of 10s, the last one a HOLD on frame 05 that
dissolves into your outro in the edit, the closing line landing over the brand
frame.

Vikram's terrace, evening, two plastic chairs. Vikram asks straight out: what is this gospel you stand on? Rahul takes one breath and says it plainly, hands still, nothing pushed across. **Staging.** Vikram's rooftop terrace. The two plastic chairs stand near the
front parapet, angled a little toward each other. At dusk the camera stands

lens side of the frame: **Rahul sits screen-left, Vikram screen-right**, each
turned in three-quarter toward the other. Behind them: the water tank
screen-left, a clothesline with clothes screen-right, the stairwell door in
the back wall between them, and beyond the back parapet the city's rooftops.
At night the camera moves **behind the chairs** looking out over the front
parapet at the city lights — seen from behind, Rahul is therefore
**screen-right** and Vikram **screen-left**. The painting sits between those two
camera positions, so the swap is never seen.
