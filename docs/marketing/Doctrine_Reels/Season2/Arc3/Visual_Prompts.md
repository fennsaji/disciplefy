Same concept, a year on: one person, one real moment, the whole reel inside it.
Seven reels predate the season's re-cut; only their arc number changed and
nothing is recorded. Generate each frame with the previous
frame attached.

Every prompt here follows the direction guide: each frame names its camera,
set, blocking and light; each clip names its transition type, one camera move,
three action beats, what must not change, and an ambient-only audio bed. Read
the **Staging** note at the top of each reel before generating — it is the map
the prompts are written against.

**Adults only.** Flow rejects anyone under 18, so every person in every frame is an adult.

**Safety rules that override composition** (from the curriculum's risk
register): no shrine, deity image or puja corner in any frame; nothing framed
for contrast with another faith; no antagonist, crowd or confrontation in any
reel about pressure at home — the only people on screen are the ones the reel
tells the viewer to go to.

One painting in this arc: the man born blind (Reel 3·1). Jesus is seen from
behind — never a face. Reel 3·2 is Deepak's — the only reel in the series led by someone other than Rahul, because a phone call is all Rahul can be to a village two hundred kilometres away, and that is the reel's point.

Timing is unchanged: five clips of 10s, the last one a HOLD on frame 05 that
dissolves into your outro in the edit, the closing line landing over the brand
frame.

## REEL 3·1 — उसने रोका क्यों नहीं? · S2·02
A hospital at night. A neighbour's young husband has been hit by a scooter, and his wife asks Rahul the question straight. Rahul has no answer that fixes it. His Bible opens at John 9 — the page becomes the man born blind — and then he just stays. Frame 03 must land on the voiceover's "यीशु ने कहा — न इसने पाप किया", never on "ज़रूर कोई पाप होगा": the painting is the refutation only if it arrives with Jesus's answer — start the bloom in the edit so the painting resolves on that line.
**Cast:** Rahul, Sunita (a neighbour, early thirties), Vinod (her husband, about thirty, asleep), a night nurse (background) · painted scene (Jesus and the blind man, Jesus from behind) · **Hook text on frame 01:** "उसने रोका क्यों नहीं?"

**Staging.** A curtained bay in a general men's ward at night. Vinod's bed runs
**across the frame**, the head of the bed **screen-left**, the foot
**screen-right**; a pale green curtain hangs along the far side behind it. The
camera stands **on the near side of the bed, in the ward aisle, looking across
the bed toward the curtain**. Sunita sits on a plastic chair on the **far side
of the bed, near the head (screen-left of centre)**, facing the bed — so she
faces the lens. Rahul starts standing on the **near side at the foot corner,
screen-right, three-quarter back to camera**, facing across the bed toward her;
after the painting he sits on a second chair on the far side, **screen-right of
Sunita**, facing the bed and the lens like her. Vinod lies on his back, asleep,
for the whole reel. A night nurse stays at a lit desk far down the aisle,
screen-right background, facing her desk.

## REEL 3·2 — माँ-बाप का आदर · S2·14
Deepak's village home. His father and he are not yet at ease since he believed. The reel is Deepak's ordinary day — tea carried to his father, the courtyard swept, a call with Rahul — and it ends with him sitting down beside his father on the charpai. No argument, no speech, no scene.

**Staging.** A mud-walled village courtyard. The house doorway is on one side,
the neem tree and the outer gate on the other. The charpai stands near the
house, set parallel to it, and **the father always sits on it facing out
toward the neem tree and the gate**. For frames 01, 02 and 05 the camera stands
**by the house, behind and to the right of the charpai, looking out toward the
neem tree** — so the father is seen three-quarter from behind, at
**screen-left**. For frames 03 and 04 the camera stands **at the far side of the
courtyard by the neem tree, looking back toward the house** — so the father,
small in the background on the charpai, faces the lens. Deepak always enters
and leaves the charpai from **screen-right**.
