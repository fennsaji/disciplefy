# Making It Clear: Why Disciplefy Exists

> "They read from the Book of the Law of God, making it clear and giving the meaning so that the people understood what was being read." — Nehemiah 8:8

## A Hunger Without Direction

The prophet Amos spoke of a strange famine — not a famine of bread, not a thirst for water, but "a famine of hearing the words of the LORD" (Amos 8:11–12). People would "stagger from sea to sea and wander from north to east, searching for the word of the LORD," and not find it.

Look around today and you will see something like that famine — but with a painful twist. The Word of God has never been more available. Most of us carry the entire Bible in our pockets, in dozens of translations, free of charge. And yet so many believers are starving. Not because Scripture is absent, but because understanding is.

Ask an honest question in any church, any fellowship, any family: *How many of us actually know how to study the Bible?* Not just read a verse and move on — but study it. Where do you begin? Genesis feels overwhelming by Leviticus. Revelation is confusing without the rest. A random verse each morning inspires for a moment but builds nothing. So many start with sincere hearts, wander for a few weeks, and quietly give up. The hunger is real. The direction is missing.

## The Scene at the Water Gate

Scripture shows us what the answer looks like. Turn to Nehemiah chapter 8.

The people of Israel were back in the land after the long exile in Babylon. A whole generation had grown up far from the temple, far from the Law, many of them now more fluent in Aramaic — the common tongue of the Babylonian world — than in the Hebrew of their fathers. The walls of Jerusalem had been rebuilt — but something more important than walls needed restoring.

So the people gathered — men, women, and all who could understand — in the square before the Water Gate. And they asked Ezra the scribe to bring out the Book of the Law of the LORD. Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people, and when he opened it, the people all stood up (Nehemiah 8:5). From daybreak until noon they listened.

But notice what made that day different. It was not only that Scripture was read. Verse 7 tells us the Levites "instructed the people in the Law while the people were standing there." And then comes verse 8, the heartbeat of the whole chapter:

**"They read from the Book of the Law of God, making it clear and giving the meaning so that the people understood what was being read."**

Three things happened that day. The Word was read. The Word was made clear — explained, opened up, quite possibly rendered into language the returned exiles could follow. And the people understood. And what was the fruit of understanding? First they wept, because the Word confronted them. Then they celebrated "with great joy, because they now understood the words that had been made known to them" (Nehemiah 8:12). Understanding produced conviction, and conviction turned into joy.

That is the pattern: not just access to Scripture, but Scripture made clear, meaning given, understanding reached. God did not leave His people alone with a scroll. He gave them teachers who walked them through it.

## The Same Question on a Desert Road

Nearly five centuries later, the pattern repeats on a desert road. An Ethiopian official is riding home from Jerusalem, reading the prophet Isaiah — earnest, hungry, holding the very Word of God in his hands. Philip runs up to the chariot and asks him, "Do you understand what you are reading?"

His answer echoes across the centuries: **"How can I, unless someone explains it to me?"** (Acts 8:30–31)

Here was a man with Scripture open in front of him — and he could not find his way through it alone. He did not need a different book. He needed a guide. So Philip "began with that very passage of Scripture and told him the good news about Jesus" (Acts 8:35). Guided understanding led him straight to Christ — and he went on his way rejoicing.

From the Water Gate to the desert road, the testimony of Scripture is consistent: God's people have always needed the Word *and* help to understand the Word. There is no shame in that need. It is how God designed the body to work.

## What Rushes Into the Vacuum

But here is the danger of our generation: when there is hunger without direction, something else rushes in to fill the vacuum.

Paul warned Timothy: "The time will come when people will not put up with sound doctrine. Instead, to suit their own desires, they will gather around them a great number of teachers to say what their itching ears want to hear. They will turn their ears away from the truth and turn aside to myths" (2 Timothy 4:3–4).

We live in that time. Never has it been easier to gather teachers around ourselves — the algorithm does the gathering for us. Sixty-second clips, viral sermons, confident voices offering fragments of truth mixed with error: prosperity promises the Bible never made, fear the Gospel never taught, shortcuts to blessing that bypass the cross. A believer who has never been grounded in the whole counsel of God cannot tell the difference. Scripture has a sobering description for that condition: "infants, tossed back and forth by the waves, and blown here and there by every wind of teaching and by the cunning and craftiness of people in their deceitful scheming" (Ephesians 4:14).

Notice — the problem is not that these believers lack sincerity. They lack roots. And roots do not grow from fragments. They grow from ordered, patient, guided study of the whole Word.

## The Berean Way

What is the answer? Not less Scripture — more Scripture, rightly handled.

Luke commends the believers in Berea with words every generation should covet: they "received the message with great eagerness and examined the Scriptures every day to see if what Paul said was true" (Acts 17:11). Daily. Systematic. Testing everything — even the apostle Paul — against the written Word. That is the posture that protects God's people from deception.

And Paul charges Timothy: "Do your best to present yourself to God as one approved, a worker who does not need to be ashamed and who correctly handles the word of truth" (2 Timothy 2:15). Correctly handling the Word is a skill — and skills are learned, step by step, with guidance.

This is, in the end, exactly what Jesus commissioned His church to do. He did not say, "Go and make converts." He said, "Go and make **disciples** of all nations… teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you" (Matthew 28:19–20). Discipleship involves teaching — teaching unto obedience. And teaching needs a path: a beginning, an order, a destination.

## Why Disciplefy

This is why Disciplefy exists.

Not to add one more feed to scroll. Not to replace your pastor, your church, or the fellowship of believers — nothing can, and nothing should. Disciplefy exists to do, in our generation, what the Levites did at the Water Gate: to take the open Book and help make it clear, give the meaning, so that people understand what is being read.

A believer opens Disciplefy with a passage or a question, and instead of a random fragment, receives a structured study — context, meaning, application, prayer — a path through the passage rather than a glance at it. Study guides grounded in sound doctrine, so the wind of every strange teaching finds less to blow around. And just as the Levites gave the sense in words the returned exiles could grasp, Disciplefy speaks in the languages of the heart — English, Hindi, Malayalam — because understanding always happens in the language you pray in.

It is a tool in the hands of the church, serving alongside her, not replacing her — so that no one who asks the Ethiopian's question, "How can I understand unless someone explains it to me?", is left without a guide.

## Come to the Water Gate

At the Water Gate, when the people finally understood the Word, they wept — and then they were sent home to celebrate, "because they now understood the words that had been made known to them."

That joy is still available. Not from another clip, another fragment, another voice telling us what our ears want to hear — but from the ancient path: the Word of God, read, made clear, and understood.

*"Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path." — Psalm 119:105*

May we be a generation that does not merely carry the Bible — but understands it, obeys it, and walks in its light.
